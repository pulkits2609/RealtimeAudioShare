Redesign RealtimeAudioShare into a polished, production-quality meeting
application inspired by the interaction quality of Discord, Google Meet
and Zoom. Use our own visual identity and adapt the design to our existing
features.

First inspect the current browser UI, shared Windows renderer, media
lifecycle, room management and tests. Then implement the redesign through
the normal application entry points.

The primary targets are browser and Windows. Keep Android interoperability
working.

1. Replace the tabs workflow with a meeting workspace

After joining, open directly into the meeting.

The main canvas should contain participant tiles and shared-content tiles.
Keep the primary controls in a persistent bottom toolbar. Put advanced
settings in anchored popovers, dialogs or side drawers.

Remove the need to switch between Meeting, Screens, Shared audio, Devices
and Connection tabs to perform ordinary actions.

Use the available window height. Scroll within drawers or long lists when
necessary. Keep the meeting canvas and primary controls accessible.

Before joining, show a concise connection screen with display name, server,
port, secure connection and credential fields. Keep advanced connection
options collapsed. Clearly indicate whether camera and microphone will
start enabled. Default both to OFF.

2. Build a persistent bottom toolbar

Provide distinct controls for:

- Microphone on/off.
- Camera on/off.
- Screen sharing.
- Device-audio sharing.
- Speaker/deafen control.
- Chat.
- Participants.
- Room management, when permitted.
- Settings.
- Leave room.

Group related controls visually. Make Leave distinct and keep it away from
frequently used controls.

Each media button must show its actual state, including starting, live,
stopping, reconnecting, permission denied or unavailable.

A small adjacent arrow can open microphone, camera or output-device
selection without toggling the main action.

Show tooltips with action names and documented shortcuts. Include visible
keyboard focus and accessible names. On small windows, move secondary
actions into a More menu while keeping essential media and Leave controls
available.

3. Treat shared content as independent tiles

Every participant gets a participant tile.

Every published screen gets its own additional tile, clearly labelled
with the owner's name and "Screen". A person sharing their camera and
screen therefore appears as two related tiles.

A system-audio share should have an additional compact source tile labelled
with its owner and "Device audio". Include playback controls, stream state
and an activity indicator derived from real audio activity where available.

Group sources by their actual owner while keeping their controls separate.
An Android companion publishing identity must appear under its parent.

Show source badges so users can distinguish camera, screen and audio.
Do not duplicate the same publication into multiple tiles.

When sharing starts or stops, update the canvas automatically.

4. Add pinning, focus and layout choices

Each participant or content tile should offer:

- Pin/unpin.
- Focus.
- Fullscreen where supported.
- Source-specific listening or quality controls where applicable.

Pinning is a local viewing preference. Support multiple pinned tiles.
Pinned tiles should stay visible while unpinned tiles occupy the remaining
gallery or filmstrip.

Provide Auto, Grid and Focus layouts. In Focus, place the selected tile
prominently with a filmstrip of other sources.

Preserve stable tile ordering. Speaking indicators should not constantly
rearrange the gallery. When a pinned source disappears, remove that pin
cleanly and select a sensible fallback without stealing keyboard focus.

Allow users to return to the full gallery in one action.

5. Make chat and participants side drawers

Chat opens on the right and smoothly reduces the canvas width. Closing it
restores that space. Keep camera and screen elements mounted where possible
so opening chat does not restart playback or reset quality settings.

The chat button should toggle the drawer. Also support a visible close
button and Escape. Restore focus to the triggering control when it closes.

Provide:

- Unread count and a restrained new-message indicator.
- Sender names and readable timestamps.
- Enter to send, Shift+Enter for a new line, and correct IME handling.
- Stable composer focus after sending.
- Clear sending and failure states based on actual delivery guarantees.
- Retry without accidentally duplicating messages.
- A "New messages" jump button when the user has scrolled upward.
- Empty and disconnected states.

The Participants drawer should show live presence, microphone/camera/share
states, speaking activity and role.

Display room membership/access separately from current live attendance.
Owner and moderator actions must update the UI automatically.

6. Improve media controls

For microphone and camera, support device selection and accurate permission
errors. Keep user intent separate from actual capture/publication state.
Turning a source OFF must stop capture immediately, including during pending
permission requests and reconnects.

Keep microphone, camera, screen video and shared device audio independent.
Make it obvious which sources are currently live.

For sharing, provide a compact setup dialog or popover containing existing
options such as quality, maximum FPS and text/motion mode.

Show requested settings and measured delivery distinctly. Viewer quality
choices must respect the publisher's available stream and layers.

Preserve per-person voice and shared-audio volume, separate playback routes,
audio-only listening, and "hear others while sharing" preferences.

Expose feedback-safe Windows capture options with concise explanations.
Show recovery when a device disappears or defaults change.

7. Add purposeful animation

Use a consistent motion system with short, restrained transitions.

Animate:

- Opening and closing side drawers.
- Gallery reflow when tiles join, leave or become pinned.
- Sharing and capture state changes.
- Popovers, tooltips and dialogs.
- Toast appearance and dismissal.
- Speaking activity and reconnect indicators.

Suggested timing is about 120–180 ms for controls and 180–250 ms for larger
layout transitions. Tune these after testing.

Avoid remounting live video elements just to animate them. Avoid expensive
effects that interfere with media rendering.

Respect prefers-reduced-motion. Under reduced motion, use simple fades or
instant changes and keep every state understandable.

8. Add configurable sound effects

Create a coherent, subtle sound set for:

- Participant join and leave.
- Incoming chat when appropriate.
- Sharing started or stopped.
- Connection lost and restored.
- Important action failure.

Provide a master interface-sounds toggle and volume control, with optional
per-event settings. Save these preferences.

Do not play notification sounds on initial history hydration, repeatedly
during retries, or for the user's own messages. Batch bursts of participant
events to avoid a cascade of sounds.

Keep notification audio separate from shared media volume. Consider its
capture route so interface sounds do not create feedback or unexpectedly
enter a device-audio stream.

Respect autoplay restrictions. Enable sounds after user interaction and
handle blocked playback gracefully.

Use original or appropriately licensed assets and include their notices.

9. Improve visual consistency

Create a coherent component system for buttons, menus, sliders, selectors,
dialogs, drawers, tooltips, badges and notifications.

Use consistent typography, spacing, radii, borders and icon sizes. Establish
clear primary, secondary, destructive and disabled states.

Support complete light and dark themes, including media placeholders,
dialogs, scrollbars, charts and focus states.

Use a recognizable AudioShare palette. Keep video and shared content as
the visual focus. Use initials or saved avatars for camera-off participants.

Provide clear empty states for being alone, having no shared content,
having no messages and having no connected camera.

10. Make recovery and errors understandable

Use concise, actionable error messages close to the relevant control.

Display reconnecting status without discarding the meeting layout or chat
draft. Preserve local viewing and listening preferences through recovery.

Avoid duplicate streams, event listeners, audio elements and participants.
Fence asynchronous operations so an old connection cannot update a new
room or reopen a stopped capture.

Distinguish a server connection failure, media connection failure, permission
failure, removed access and deleted room.

Use bounded notifications with deduplication. Announce important state
changes accessibly without making every transient event disruptive.

11. Accessibility and responsive behavior

Support keyboard navigation throughout, logical focus order, dialog focus
containment, Escape dismissal and focus restoration.

Provide sufficient contrast, usable pointer targets, accessible control
names, pressed/expanded states and screen-reader announcements.

Handle browser zoom, narrow desktop windows and mobile browser layouts.
Keep chat and essential controls usable with the on-screen keyboard open.

Document shortcuts and avoid collisions with text editing or operating
system shortcuts.

12. Preserve the deployment contract

Keep run.bat 9999 9998 9996 9997 compatible.

Keep the public API/signaling at https://vps.pulkitworks.info:9999,
the optional frontend at https://vps.pulkitworks.info:9998,
media UDP 9996 and TCP fallback 9997.

Preserve the relative /media URL, existing /v1 request/response contracts,
owner/invitation authentication, room tokens and Android companion publishing.

Preserve exact-origin CORS, Electron null-origin support and secret-gated
visitor-IP handling.

Keep microphone, device audio, camera and screen video as distinct tracks.
Keep chat on the reliable WebRTC data channel.

Keep credentials and existing access data intact across upgrades.

13. Verify the experience before packaging

Test owner and guest joining, invitation creation and usage, room changes,
normal leave, forced removal, role changes and reconnects.

Verify automatic roster and tile updates without refresh.

Test simultaneous camera, microphone, screen and device audio. Verify
independent stop controls, pending capture cancellation, device switching
and permission rejection.

Test multiple simultaneous sharers, pinning, fullscreen, quality selection,
chat drawer reflow, unread messages and keyboard focus.

Verify that opening drawers and changing layouts do not interrupt media.

Use isolated HTTPS/WSS testing with two real clients exchanging audio and
chat. Verify UDP and repeat with UDP unavailable to exercise TCP fallback.

Measure UI responsiveness and media CPU usage with several participants.
Report the tested scenarios and remaining hardware/network gaps.

Deliver the working implementation, screenshots of the main layouts,
verification results and a Windows build ready for acceptance testing.
Prepare the Store update package after those checks pass.
